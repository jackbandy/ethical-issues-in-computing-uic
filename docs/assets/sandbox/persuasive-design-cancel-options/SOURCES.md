# Sources & Licenses — "Persuasive Design" cancel-flow screenshot options

NOTICE: These files were collected by an LLM-based coding system on 2026-10-28 as candidate replacements for the Fogg Behavior Model diagram on the Week 10, Day 2 "Persuasive Design" slide: real examples of an annoying "tap to cancel" flow. Not yet reviewed. Each was rendered at 300 dpi from an FTC document (fetched through the Wayback Machine, since ftc.gov refuses plain fetches), cropped to the exhibit. The FTC's documents are U.S. government works and in the public domain; the app screens they reproduce remain the companies' own designs, shown here as evidence in a public enforcement action, so treat them as classroom commentary. Quotations below are from the documents themselves.

## FTC v. Uber Technologies (Uber One), N.D. Cal. No. 3:25-cv-03477, complaint filed April 21, 2025

Source: [ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/uberonecomplaint.pdf](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/uberonecomplaint.pdf) ([Wayback copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20261003082008/https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/uberonecomplaint.pdf)). The complaint says canceling took "at least 7 screens that require at least 12 different actions."

- **01-uber-one-cancellation-loop.png** — Ex. 20, complaint p. 24 — 1550×1247 — four cancellation screens in a row with a red arrow looping from the last back to the first; one consumer called it "a circular loop" that was "impossibly difficult to navigate." Chosen for the slide: rebuilt with CTA-style arrows in `docs/assets/uber-cancel-loop/` (see `make_figure.py`)

- **02-uber-one-contact-support-to-cancel.png** — Ex. 19, p. 23 — 600×1222 — "You could save $25 each month" with only a "Close" button; the small print says the next payment "may be in process. Please contact support to cancel." Per the complaint, "Close" sends consumers back to the start of the flow

- **03-uber-one-90-percent-off-if-you-stay.png** — Ex. 18, p. 22 — 600×1230 — "Get 90% off your next month if you stay ... We'd hate to see you leave, Morgan," with a bright "Claim offer" button above a dark "End Uber One." Shows a first name (Morgan) from the screenshot as filed

- **04-uber-one-pause-instead.png** — Ex. 17, p. 21 — 600×1230 — "Want to pause your membership instead?" with "Pause Uber One" in the prominent button and "No thanks" below it; the complaint notes the buttons are "flipped from their placement on the previous screen," so tapping the same spot pauses instead of canceling
