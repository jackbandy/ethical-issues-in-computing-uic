# Branding — what these files are and where they come from

> _Note: this file was drafted by an LLM-based coding system._

Everything here is generated by `sandbox/make_branding.py`. Edit that script and re-run it
from `docs/`; don't hand-edit the outputs.

    python3 assets/branding/sandbox/make_branding.py

## loop-icon.svg, favicon.ico, apple-touch-icon.png, icon-192.png, icon-512.png

The tab and home-screen icon: the Blue Line where it runs through the Loop — the
Milwaukee–Dearborn diagonal, the turn east under Lake, and the hook south past Washington
and Jackson before the line heads west toward Forest Park.

It is a **crop, not a redrawing**. The script pulls the route path straight out of
`assets/blue-line-full-path-tweak-divided.svg`, the same map the schedule is built on, and
squares a viewBox around the downtown hook. The station circles are dropped: at 16px they
read as grit rather than stops. The hook is the one stretch of the route that could not be
mistaken for another CTA line, which is the whole job of an icon at that size.

`favicon.ico` (written to the site root) carries 16/32/48/64/128px layers, each rendered
from the vector rather than resampled off one bitmap. The PWA icons are composited on white,
since Android puts home-screen icons on its own plate and a transparent one can vanish
against a dark wallpaper. Stroke color is `#00A1DE`, the CTA Blue Line blue that
`css/index.css` uses.

Referenced by `_includes/icons.html`, `site.webmanifest`, and — with paths relative to
`slides/` — `slides/includes/head-icons.html`.

## blue-line-og.png

The site-wide preview card (`og:image` / `twitter:image`, configured in `_config.yml` under
`social:` and emitted by `_includes/social-meta.html`). 1200 × 630.

One car and one line: the ETHICS half of the two-car wordmark train in
`assets/trains/blue-line-ethics-masthead-v4c.svg`, resting on the same blue rail it rests on
at the top of every page. A link preview should look like the top of the page it opens, not
like a second logo — and at the size a preview actually gets shown, one word on a line still
reads where a sentence would not.

The script re-frames the source SVG's viewBox onto the second car (`x` 1246–2402 of its
2440-unit canvas) rather than cutting a new file, so the drawing is never duplicated. The car
is then placed by measuring its ink, not its canvas — the masthead frame carries slack above
the roof and below the wheels — and the rail's top edge is set three pixels inside the bottom
of the wheels so the car rests on the line instead of floating over it.
